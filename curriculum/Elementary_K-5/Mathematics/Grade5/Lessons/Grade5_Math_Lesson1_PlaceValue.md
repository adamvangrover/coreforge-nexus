# Lesson 1: Place Value to Billions and Thousandths

## Lesson Overview
Students will explore the base-ten place value system, extending their understanding to billions (whole numbers) and thousandths (decimals). They will understand that a digit in one place represents 10 times as much as it represents in the place to its right and 1/10 of what it represents in the place to its left.

## Learning Objectives
By the end of this lesson, students will be able to:
1.  Identify the place value of digits in multi-digit numbers (up to billions and down to thousandths).
2.  Explain the "10 times" and "1/10" relationship between adjacent place values.
3.  Read and write numbers in standard, word, and expanded forms.

## Standards Alignment
- **NY-5.NBT.1**: Recognize that in a multi-digit number, a digit in one place represents 10 times as much as it represents in the place to its right and 1/10 of what it represents in the place to its left.

## Materials Needed
- Base-ten blocks
- Place value charts (laminated for dry-erase use)
- Dice (10-sided for generating digits)
- Whiteboards

## Lesson Procedure

### 1. Warm-up (10 mins)
- **Problem of the Day**: Write the number 4,520 on the board. Ask: "What is the value of the 5? What is the value of the 2?"
- Discuss: "What happens if we move the 5 one spot to the left? One spot to the right?"

### 2. Direct Instruction (20 mins)
- **The Place Value Chart**: Draw a large chart on the board:
  `... Millions | Hundred Thousands | Ten Thousands | Thousands | Hundreds | Tens | Ones . Tenths | Hundredths | Thousandths`
- **Concept Demo**: Show a "One" cube. Show a "Ten" rod (10 ones). Show a "Hundred" flat (10 tens).
- **The Rule of 10**: Explain that every jump to the left is $\times 10$, and every jump to the right is $\div 10$ (or $\times \frac{1}{10}$).
- **Decimals**: Introduce Tenths as taking a "One" and cutting it into 10 slices. Hundredths is cutting a Tenth into 10 slices.

### 3. Guided Practice (15 mins)
- **Number Builder**: Give students a number verbally: "Three million, four hundred two thousand, and five tenths."
- Students write it on their place value charts.
- Ask: "What digit is in the ten-thousands place?"
- **Relationship Practice**: Write `444`. Ask: "How does the 4 in the tens place compare to the 4 in the ones place?" (It is 10 times greater). "How does it compare to the 4 in the hundreds place?" (It is 1/10 the value).

### 4. Independent Practice (10 mins)
- Worksheet: "Place Value Detectives".
    - Convert Standard Form to Expanded Form: $34.56 \rightarrow 30 + 4 + 0.5 + 0.06$.
    - Word Problems: "A city has a population of 1,230,000. Another city has 1/10th of that population. What is the population of the second city?"

### 5. Assessment & Wrap-up (5 mins)
- **Exit Ticket**:
    1. Write 5,203.4 in expanded form.
    2. Fill in the blank: The value of the digit in the hundreds place is _____ times the value of the digit in the tens place.

## Resources & References
- **Differentiation**:
    - *Visual Aid*: Keep the Place Value Chart visible at all times.
    - *Challenge*: Ask students to describe the relationship between the Millions place and the Thousands place ($\times 1000$).
