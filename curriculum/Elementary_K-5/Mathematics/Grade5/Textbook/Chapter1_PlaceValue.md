# Chapter 1: Place Value

## 1.1 The Base-Ten System
Our number system is based on the number 10. Each place value is 10 times greater than the place to its right.

| Millions | Hundred Thousands | Ten Thousands | Thousands | Hundreds | Tens | Ones | . | Tenths | Hundredths | Thousandths |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| 1,000,000 | 100,000 | 10,000 | 1,000 | 100 | 10 | 1 | . | 0.1 | 0.01 | 0.001 |

### The "Ten Times" Rule
*   $10 \text{ ones} = 1 \text{ ten}$
*   $10 \text{ tens} = 1 \text{ hundred}$
*   $10 \text{ hundreds} = 1 \text{ thousand}$

### The "One-Tenth" Rule
*   $1 \text{ ten} = \frac{1}{10} \text{ of a hundred}$
*   $1 \text{ one} = \frac{1}{10} \text{ of a ten}$
*   $0.1 = \frac{1}{10} \text{ of } 1$

## 1.2 Reading and Writing Decimals
There are three ways to write numbers:
1.  **Standard Form**: Using digits. (e.g., 5.32)
2.  **Word Form**: Using words. (e.g., "Five and thirty-two hundredths").
    *   *Tip*: The decimal point is read as "and".
3.  **Expanded Form**: Showing the value of each digit. (e.g., $5 + 0.3 + 0.02$).

## 1.3 Comparing Decimals
To compare decimals, line up the decimal points!
*   Compare 0.4 and 0.42.
    *   0.40
    *   0.42
    *   0.42 is greater because it has 2 hundredths, while 0.40 has 0 hundredths.

## 1.4 Rounding Decimals
**Rules for Rounding**:
1.  Underline the digit you are rounding to.
2.  Look at the neighbor to the right.
3.  If the neighbor is 5 or more, round up (add 1).
4.  If the neighbor is 4 or less, stay the same.
5.  Drop everything to the right.

*Example*: Round 3.14159 to the nearest hundredth.
*   Digit: 4 (Hundredths place).
*   Neighbor: 1 (Thousandths place).
*   1 is small, so 4 stays the same.
*   Answer: 3.14.
