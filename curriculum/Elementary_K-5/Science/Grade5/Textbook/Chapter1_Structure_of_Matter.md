# Chapter 1: Structure and Properties of Matter

## 1.1 What is Matter?
Matter is anything that has mass and takes up space (volume). Everything around you is made of matter—the air you breathe, the water you drink, and the chair you sit on.

### The Three States of Matter
1.  **Solids**: Have a definite shape and a definite volume. The particles are packed tightly together and vibrate in place.
    *   *Example*: Ice, Rock, Wood.
2.  **Liquids**: Have a definite volume but no definite shape. They take the shape of their container. The particles can slide past each other.
    *   *Example*: Water, Milk, Oil.
3.  **Gases**: Have no definite shape and no definite volume. They expand to fill their container. The particles move fast and are far apart.
    *   *Example*: Oxygen, Helium, Steam.

## 1.2 The Particle Model
Scientists use the **Particle Model** to explain how matter behaves. Imagine that all matter is made of tiny, invisible balls called particles (atoms and molecules).

*   **In Solids**: Particles hold hands tightly. They can't move around, just wiggle.
*   **In Liquids**: Particles hold hands loosely. They can dance around each other.
*   **In Gases**: Particles don't hold hands at all. They fly around freely!

## 1.3 Properties of Matter
We can describe matter by its properties. Some properties we can see or measure:
*   **Color**: Is it red, blue, or clear?
*   **Hardness**: Can it scratch other things?
*   **Reflectivity**: Does it shine like a mirror?
*   **Magnetism**: Does a magnet stick to it?
*   **Solubility**: Does it dissolve in water? (Like sugar or salt).

## 1.4 Mixtures and Solutions
*   **Mixture**: Two or more substances mixed together but not chemically combined. You can usually separate them again.
    *   *Example*: Trail mix (nuts and raisins).
*   **Solution**: A special kind of mixture where one substance dissolves into another and looks the same throughout.
    *   *Example*: Salt water.

## 1.5 Conservation of Mass
No matter what you do—mix it, heat it, freeze it—the amount of matter stays the same. This is called the **Law of Conservation of Matter**. If you melt an ice cube, the water weighs the same as the ice did!
