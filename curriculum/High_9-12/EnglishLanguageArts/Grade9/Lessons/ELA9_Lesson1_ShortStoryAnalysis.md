# Lesson 1: Introduction to Short Story Analysis

## Lesson Overview
This lesson introduces students to the fundamental elements of fiction: plot, setting, character, conflict, and theme. Students will analyze these elements using a short animated film ("The Present" or similar) as a scaffold before applying them to a written text.

## Learning Objectives
By the end of this lesson, students will be able to:
1.  Define the five elements of fiction (Plot, Setting, Character, Conflict, Theme).
2.  Identify the stages of a plot diagram (Exposition, Rising Action, Climax, Falling Action, Resolution).
3.  Analyze a simple narrative to identify these elements.

## Standards Alignment
- **NYS Next Gen 9-10R3**: Analyze how complex characters develop over the course of a text, interact with other characters, and advance the plot or develop the theme.

## Materials Needed
- Projector/Screen
- Video: "The Present" (Short film)
- Handout: Blank Plot Diagram
- Whiteboard

## Lesson Procedure

### 1. Warm-up (5 mins)
- **Quick Write**: "Think of your favorite movie. In 3 sentences, describe what happens."
- Share out: Point out that students are describing the **Plot**.

### 2. Direct Instruction (15 mins)
- **Mini-Lecture**: Define the 5 Elements.
    - *Setting*: Time and place.
    - *Character*: Protagonist vs. Antagonist. Static vs. Dynamic.
    - *Conflict*: Internal (Man vs. Self) vs. External (Man vs. Man, Nature, Society).
    - *Theme*: The underlying message.
    - *Plot*: Freytag's Pyramid (Draw on board: Exposition -> Rising Action -> Climax -> Falling Action -> Resolution).

### 3. Modeling (10 mins)
- **Watch**: Play the short film "The Present" (approx 4 mins).
- **Group Discussion**: Fill out the Plot Diagram together on the board.
    - *Exposition*: Boy playing video games, mom brings box.
    - *Inciting Incident*: Opens box, finds dog with three legs.
    - *Rising Action*: Kicks dog away, dog persists, boy warms up.
    - *Climax*: Boy stands up (reveals he has one leg).
    - *Resolution*: Boy and dog go outside to play.

### 4. Guided Practice (10 mins)
- **Think-Pair-Share**:
    - "What is the Theme of this story?" (e.g., Acceptance, Resilience, Don't judge a book by its cover).
    - "What type of conflict is present?" (Man vs. Self - dealing with his disability).

### 5. Assessment & Wrap-up (5 mins)
- **Exit Ticket**:
    - Draw a quick plot diagram and label the 5 parts.
    - Define "Climax".

## Resources & References
- **Differentiation**:
    - *Visual Learners*: The video serves as a visual scaffold.
    - *Advanced*: Ask students to identify "Foreshadowing" in the video (e.g., the blinds being closed representing his isolation).
