# Unit 1: Short Stories and Literary Elements

## Overview and Alignment
This unit introduces students to the rigorous analysis of literature expected in high school. Through the study of classic and contemporary short stories, students will master the five elements of fiction: plot, character, setting, conflict, and theme. They will learn to cite textual evidence to support their claims.

**NYS Next Generation Standards:**
- **9-10R1**: Cite strong and thorough textual evidence to support analysis of what the text says explicitly as well as inferences drawn from the text.
- **9-10R3**: Analyze how complex characters develop over the course of a text.

## Core Concepts / Units
1.  **Plot Structure**: Freytag's Pyramid (Exposition, Rising Action, Climax, Falling Action, Resolution).
2.  **Characterization**: Direct vs. Indirect Characterization (STEAL: Speech, Thoughts, Effect on others, Actions, Looks).
3.  **Conflict**: Internal vs. External conflicts.
4.  **Theme**: Identifying the universal message.
5.  **Irony**: Verbal, Situational, and Dramatic Irony.

## Instructional Strategies & Resources
- **Close Reading**: Annotating texts for literary devices.
- **Graphic Organizers**: Plot Diagrams, Character Maps.
- **Resources**:
    - "The Most Dangerous Game" by Richard Connell.
    - "The Gift of the Magi" by O. Henry.
    - "The Necklace" by Guy de Maupassant.

## Assessment Strategies
- **Formative**: Annotation checks, Reading quizzes.
- **Summative**:
    - Literary Analysis Paragraph (Character Analysis).
    - Unit 1 Test: Terminology and Reading Comprehension.
