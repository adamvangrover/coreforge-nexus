# Chapter 1: Foundations of Algebra

## Section 1.1: Variables and Expressions

### Introduction
Algebra is a language of symbols. In arithmetic, we use numbers to represent specific quantities (like 5 apples). In algebra, we use **variables**—usually letters like $x$ or $y$—to represent unknown quantities or quantities that can change.

### Key Definitions
- **Variable**: A symbol used to represent a number.
- **Algebraic Expression**: A combination of variables, numbers, and at least one operation (e.g., $3x + 2$).
- **Evaluate**: To find the value of an expression by substituting a number for the variable.

### Translating Words to Math
| Operation | Key Words | Example | Algebraic Expression |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| Addition (+) | Sum, plus, increased by, more than | The sum of a number and 5 | $n + 5$ |
| Subtraction (-) | Difference, minus, decreased by, less than | 4 less than a number | $n - 4$ |
| Multiplication (x) | Product, times, of, twice | The product of 3 and a number | $3n$ |
| Division (÷) | Quotient, divided by, ratio | The quotient of a number and 2 | $\frac{n}{2}$ |

### Example 1: Evaluation
Evaluate the expression $2x - 4$ when $x = 5$.
**Solution**:
1. Substitute 5 for $x$: $2(5) - 4$
2. Multiply: $10 - 4$
3. Subtract: $6$

## Section 1.2: Solving Linear Equations

### The Golden Rule of Algebra
**"Whatever you do to one side of the equation, you must do to the other."**
To solve an equation, our goal is to **isolate** the variable. We do this using **inverse operations**.

- The inverse of Addition is **Subtraction**.
- The inverse of Subtraction is **Addition**.
- The inverse of Multiplication is **Division**.
- The inverse of Division is **Multiplication**.

### Example 2: One-Step Equation
Solve: $x + 7 = 12$
**Solution**:
1. We want $x$ alone. 7 is added to $x$.
2. Subtract 7 from both sides: $x + 7 - 7 = 12 - 7$
3. Result: $x = 5$

### Example 3: Two-Step Equation
Solve: $3y - 5 = 16$
**Solution**:
1. Undo addition/subtraction first. Add 5 to both sides.
   $$3y = 21$$
2. Undo multiplication/division. Divide by 3.
   $$\frac{3y}{3} = \frac{21}{3}$$
3. Result: $y = 7$

## Section 1.3: Real World Applications
**Problem**: A gym charges a $20 membership fee plus $5 per visit. How many visits can you make for $45?
**Model**: Let $v$ = number of visits.
Cost equation: $5v + 20 = 45$
**Solve**:
1. Subtract 20: $5v = 25$
2. Divide by 5: $v = 5$
**Answer**: You can make 5 visits.
